**Current State**: Generic content management system (pages, blogs, FAQs)

## Part 1: Why Generic CMS is Not Enough for JVTO

### The Problem

A generic CMS assumes:
- Content is mostly freeform
- Routes are simple (create → edit → publish)
- Assets are just images/files
- Reviews are social proof only
- Crew is operational data only

### JVTO's Reality (from SSOT)

JVTO's SSOT contains:
- ✅ Route ownership & canonical alignment
- ✅ Claim-to-evidence mapping
- ✅ Verification credentials & legal proof
- ✅ Trust architecture & narrative pillars
- ✅ Crew registry (operational + public personas)
- ✅ Review logic & evidence linking
- ✅ Structured content families (5+ page types)
- ✅ AI-readable messaging variants

**Implication**: Generic CMS will create:
- ❌ Broken route logic
- ❌ Inconsistent brand messaging
- ❌ Weak GEO/AEO output
- ❌ Disconnected proof pages
- ❌ Underused strategic assets

## Part 2: Core System Architecture Recommendation

### Layer 1: Canonical Content Registry

**Purpose**: Manage main page families while preserving canonical structure

**Modules**:
- Core Pages (home, about, etc.)
- Verify Pages (proof & evidence pages)
- Travel Pages (how-to, guides)
- Policy Pages (terms, booking policy)
- Destination Pages (Mount Bromo, Ijen, etc.)
- Tour Pages (tour offerings)

**Key Controls**:
- Route ownership enforcement
- Metadata consistency (H1, title, meta description)
- Cross-link integrity
- Section structure validation
- Content ownership assignment

---

### Layer 2: Trust & Proof Management

**Purpose**: Control the strongest JVTO differentiator

**Modules**:
- Verification Credentials (licenses, certifications)
- Proof Assets (permits, certificates, evidence media)
- Press & Recognition (media mentions, awards)
- Partners / Authority Signals
- Claim Map (claims ↔ evidence ↔ pages)

**Key Controls**:
- Claim-to-evidence linking
- Evidence validity tracking
- Proof page generation
- Authority signal management
- Legal credential versioning

---

### Layer 3: Asset Intelligence

**Purpose**: Smart asset management that serves multiple channels

**Modules**:
- Asset Library (with classification)
- Asset Categories (proof, content, marketing, internal)
- Asset Roles (destination hero, crew profile, proof, ad material)
- Related Entity Links (destination, crew, proof page, tour)
- Alt Text Management (standardized, SEO-optimized)
- File Naming Standardization
- Size / Variant Management
- Usage Control (public vs internal)

**Key Classification**:
```
Proof Assets     → Legal, permits, certificates
Content Assets   → Destination imagery, guides
Marketing Assets → Ad materials, social
Internal Assets  → Team photos, ops docs
```

**Key Benefit**: One asset can be correctly reused across:
- Destination pages
- Verify pages
- Blog posts
- Ads
- Crew pages
- Review cards
- Schema support

---

### Layer 4: Narrative & GEO/AEO Control

**Purpose**: Control brand voice & AI-readable consistency

**Modules**:
- Core Brand Thesis
- Narrative Pillars
- Claim Map (with NLP variants)
- Snippet Library (AI-facing, CS-facing, customer-facing)
- Messaging Variants
- Channel Adaptation Rules

**Key Distinction**: CMS does NOT generate content blindly.  
CMS CONTROLS:
- Which claims are active
- Which variants are official
- Which snippets are approved
- Which proof supports them
- How they adapt for: web, FAQ, support, ads, AI

---

### Layer 5: Review Registry

**Purpose**: Reviews as evidence layer, not just testimonials

**Modules**:
- Review Source (Google, TripAdvisor, etc.)
- Review Platform Management
- Review Theme Classification
- Crew Mention Tracking
- Route/Destination Relevance
- Public Usability Assessment
- Content Suitability (website, ads, crew pages, proof)

**Key Insight**: Reviews are:
- Social proof ✓
- Evidence ✓
- Content source material ✓
- Crew validation ✓
- Ad angle input ✓
- GEO/AEO support ✓

---

### Layer 6: Crew Registry (Split Design)

**Public Crew Registry**:
- Team pages
- Public trust building
- Language/role display
- Route expertise
- Review mentions
- Profile media
- Public bio & self quote

**Internal Crew Ops** (separate):
- Phone & WhatsApp contacts
- Assignment logic
- Scheduling
- Availability
- Internal notes
- Ops-only fields

⚠️ **Critical**: Do NOT merge into one form. Separate modules for data clarity.

## Part 3: Derivative & Support Systems

### Layer 7-10: Derivative Outputs (Output Layer, Not Core)

**Blog Support**
- Constrained by official entities, claims, proof
- Uses narrative pillars
- Links back to source pages
- Maintains SSOT alignment

**Ads Content Support**
- Derives from narrative system
- Uses approved messaging variants
- Tied to proof architecture
- Prevents off-brand angles

**FAQ Support**
- Generated from claim map
- Uses approved snippets
- Links to verification
- Maintains consistency

**AI Snippet / CS Reply Support**
- Uses official AI-facing summaries
- Maintains brand voice
- Version-controlled

## Part 4: What NOT to Force Too Early

### ❌ Do NOT Turn CMS into Full Operations Platform

Keep SEPARATE (for now):
- Booking operations
- Transport assignment
- Hotel reservations
- Crew scheduling
- Finance & expenses
- Procurement workflows

*(These can connect later, but overload kills focus)*

### ❌ Do NOT Make Blog the Center

Blog is an OUTPUT LAYER, not the center.  
Center remains: SSOT → Narrative → Proof → Assets → Routes

### ❌ Do NOT Allow Unrestricted Route Editing

Routes are strategic. Implement guardrails:
- Protected core routes (home, policies, destinations)
- Review workflow for route changes
- Slug conflict detection
- Canonical alignment validation
