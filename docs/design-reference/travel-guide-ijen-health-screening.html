Reading time · 6 min · Medical reference

Ijen access rules can require a recent local health certificate. This is a regulatory requirement set by BBKSDA — the government conservation authority for Kawah Ijen — not a rule JVTO invents. When it applies, JVTO coordinates the clinic workflow with a named, SIP-licensed physician, and the certificate is QR-verified at the crater gate.

The rule is BBKSDA's, not ours

The screening requirement is set by BBKSDA Surat Edaran SE.1658/KSA.9/2024 and reflected in the public crater-access ticket terms at tiket.bbksdajatim.org, which require a health certificate from a doctor for crater-zone entry when conditions meet the threshold. JVTO's role is to coordinate the clinic workflow so you can satisfy that requirement — not to impose it. That distinction matters: we are the logistics layer on a government safety rule, not the authority behind it.

Why screening exists

Kawah Ijen is a 2,386-metre active volcano with a sulfur lake at its crater. Sulfur dioxide and hydrogen sulfide are released continuously from the crater floor, the night ascent runs from roughly 01:00 to 04:00, and the air at the rim can change from breathable to dangerous within minutes when the wind shifts. The screening is designed to catch the conditions that turn this environment into a medical emergency — before you are standing on the rim.

The four-step protocol

When access rules require it, the workflow runs through the Ijen Digital Health Security System:

  1. Pre-hike health check
    JVTO coordinates the check at a partner clinic, or as a session at your hotel or the JVTO office, the evening before your hike.
  2. Vitals recorded digitally
    Your readings are entered into the digital system — not a handwritten note.
  3. QR-verified certificate issued
    A surat sehat (health clearance) is issued by Dr. Ahmad Irwandanu, carrying a QR code tied to his licence.
  4. Scanned at the crater gate
    BBKSDA staff scan the QR code at the access gate. The certificate is checked, not just issued and ignored.

The hard gate

No valid QR code = no access to the crater zone. This is a BBKSDA rule. We will not take you up without it.

If you are assessed unfit

It is rare but possible. If the screening determines you should not ascend, the climb is cancelled for that individual, not the group — you can wait at base camp or a safe zone while the rest of your party continues. The screening session cost is non-refundable. This is a BBKSDA safety decision, not JVTO discretion.

Why this is not bureaucratic theater

Between 2015 and 2026, four tourists died at Kawah Ijen. Two of the four were fitness-related: a 68-year-old visitor in 2015 (exhaustion and respiratory distress while ascending), and a 64-year-old visitor in 2023 (a cardiac event near the upper trail, who collapsed despite supplemental oxygen). Both involved cardiovascular or respiratory failure in people who lacked the fitness for the climb — exactly the pattern an SpO₂, blood-pressure, heart-rate and respiratory check is designed to catch. The screening targets a documented, recurring cause of death at this specific volcano.

What you should do before the trip

  1. Disclose any pre-existing cardiovascular or respiratory condition in your inquiry form.
  2. Bring any home-country medical paperwork relevant to altitude or respiratory conditions.
  3. Get a good night of sleep before the screening session.
  4. Avoid alcohol the night before — it can register as elevated blood pressure.
Verify before you book

You can check Dr. Irwandanu's licence at the Kemenkes SatuSehat and KKI registries linked above, and read the regulatory and police-safety context on the Police & Safety proof page. The certificate behind your climb is auditable — by design.
